The Process

How to Get a GLP-1 Prescription the Safe Way

Getting a GLP-1 medicine without a clinician's evaluation is a red flag. Here's what a legitimate, safe process generally looks like.

1

Find a Licensed Prescriber

Start with your primary care provider, an endocrinologist, an obesity medicine specialist, or a telehealth clinician licensed in your state.

2

Complete a Health Evaluation

Share your health history, goals and current medicines. The clinician checks contraindications and may order labs. This is reviewed by a licensed clinician, not a chatbot.

3

Prescription to a Licensed Pharmacy

If a GLP-1 medicine is appropriate, the prescription goes to a state-licensed pharmacy. You're told about risks, the titration schedule and what to expect.

4

Follow-Up & Titration

Your prescriber checks in on side effects and progress and adjusts the dose step by step. Ongoing follow-up is a sign of real clinical care.

Your Options

Telehealth vs. In-Person Care

Both routes can be legitimate. What matters is a licensed prescriber, a real evaluation and a licensed pharmacy.

In-Person Visit

With your primary care provider, an endocrinologist or an obesity medicine specialist.

  • Physical exam and labs in one place
  • Your full medical record is already on file
  • Easier coordination with your other medicines and conditions
  • Insurance billing and prior authorization through a familiar office

Telehealth Visit

A video visit and/or health questionnaire with a remote clinician.

  • Convenient if you have limited access to specialists
  • The clinician must be licensed in the state where you are located
  • Should include a real review of your history and medicines
  • Should offer follow-up and a way to reach the care team with side-effect questions
Verify Before You Fill

How to Check a Pharmacy Is State-Licensed

A few minutes of checking can protect you from counterfeit or unapproved products.

Look It Up With Your State Board of Pharmacy

Your state board of pharmacy typically offers an online license lookup. Confirm the pharmacy holds an active license, including in your state if it ships to you.

Confirm the Basics

A legitimate pharmacy requires a valid prescription, has a licensed pharmacist available to answer questions, and lists a physical U.S. address and phone number.

Use National Resources

The National Association of Boards of Pharmacy (NABP) offers resources for checking online pharmacies, and the FDA publishes consumer guidance on buying medicines online safely.

What Good Care Looks Like

A Legitimate Process
Looks Like This.

    A licensed clinician reviews your history, medicines and goals before anything is prescribed, and is willing to say no if it isn't a fit.

  • Oversight at Every Step

    Side-effect management, titration adjustments and check-ins are part of the plan, not an afterthought.

  • A Licensed Pharmacy

    Your prescription is filled by a state-licensed pharmacy, with a pharmacist you can reach and clear labeling and storage instructions.

  • Lifestyle Guidance

    GLP-1 medicines work best alongside nutrition and activity changes. Good care includes practical guidance, not just a prescription.

Questions to Ask Your Prescriber

  1. Am I a good candidate for a GLP-1 medicine, and why?
  2. Which FDA-approved brand do you recommend for me?
  3. What is my starting dose and titration schedule?
  4. Which side effects mean I should contact you?
  5. Which pharmacy will fill this, and is it licensed in my state?
  6. Is it covered by my insurance, and is prior authorization needed?
  7. How often will we follow up?
Protect Yourself

Red Flags With Online GLP-1 Sellers

If you see any of these, walk away and talk to a licensed clinician instead.

No Prescription Required

GLP-1 medicines are prescription-only in the U.S. "No doctor needed" or "no prescription required" means the seller is operating illegally.

No Verifiable License

No licensed pharmacist, no pharmacy license you can look up with a state board of pharmacy, and no physical U.S. address.

Counterfeit or Unapproved Products

The FDA has warned consumers about counterfeit GLP-1 products and about unapproved or compounded versions sold online. Ask for an FDA-approved brand dispensed by a licensed pharmacy.

"Research Use Only" Labels

Products labeled "for research use only" or "not for human consumption" are not medicines approved for you to take.

Too-Good-to-Be-True Prices

Prices far below typical costs, "limited-time" countdowns and pressure to buy quickly are common tactics of illegitimate sellers.

Unusual Payment Methods

Requests to pay only by cryptocurrency, gift cards or wire transfer are a warning sign. Legitimate pharmacies use standard, traceable payment.
