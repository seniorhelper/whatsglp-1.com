// Quick Answer

What Is GLP-1?

GLP-1 stands for glucagon-like peptide-1, a hormone your body naturally produces in your gut after eating. It signals your brain that you're full, slows digestion, and helps regulate blood sugar by stimulating insulin release. GLP-1 receptor agonist medicines are lab-developed compounds that mimic this hormone, giving your body more of its natural "stop eating" signal under a clinician's supervision.

The Science, Simply Explained

Your Body Already Makes GLP-1.
Here's Why That Matters.

GLP-1 isn't a new invention. It's a hormone your body has always produced. The breakthrough is learning how to harness it therapeutically.

01

You Eat a Meal

Your intestinal L-cells detect the nutrients. They begin releasing GLP-1 into your bloodstream within minutes of eating.

02

GLP-1 Signals the Brain

GLP-1 acts on the hypothalamus, the brain's appetite control center, activating receptors that send a satiety signal. You feel full sooner.

03

Blood Sugar Stabilizes

GLP-1 stimulates insulin release in response to food, but mainly when blood sugar is elevated. This glucose-dependent action makes it very precise.

The problem? In people with obesity or metabolic dysfunction, the natural GLP-1 response is often blunted or too short-lived. GLP-1 medicines fill that gap, giving your biology a longer-lasting version of a signal it may not be producing effectively on its own.

Plain-English Glossary

GLP-1 Terminology Decoded

The world of GLP-1 comes with a lot of jargon. Here's what the most important terms actually mean, in plain English.

GLP-1 Receptor Agonist

A medicine that activates GLP-1 receptors in the body, just like the natural hormone does. "Agonist" simply means it binds to and activates the receptor. Examples include semaglutide and tirzepatide.

Semaglutide

A GLP-1 receptor agonist sold as Ozempic (weekly injection for type 2 diabetes), Wegovy (weekly injection for chronic weight management) and Rybelsus (daily tablet for type 2 diabetes).

Tirzepatide

A dual-action medicine (GLP-1 and GIP receptor agonist) sold as Mounjaro and Zepbound. Studies suggest tirzepatide may produce greater average weight loss than semaglutide alone.

FDA-Approved Brand

A medicine the U.S. Food and Drug Administration has reviewed for safety, effectiveness and manufacturing quality for specific uses. Ozempic, Wegovy, Rybelsus, Mounjaro and Zepbound are FDA-approved brand-name GLP-1 medicines.

Incretin Hormone

A class of gut-derived hormones, including GLP-1 and GIP, that stimulate insulin secretion after eating. GLP-1 is the most well-studied incretin and is the basis for an entire class of modern metabolic medicines.

Gastric Emptying

The rate at which food moves from your stomach into the small intestine. GLP-1 medicines slow this process, which contributes to prolonged feelings of fullness and a more gradual rise in blood sugar after meals.

Insulin Sensitivity

How effectively your cells respond to insulin to absorb glucose. Poor insulin sensitivity (insulin resistance) is common in obesity and type 2 diabetes. GLP-1 medicines can help improve this over time.

Titration / Dose Escalation

The process of starting at a low dose and gradually increasing over weeks to months. This approach helps minimize side effects (especially nausea) while allowing the body to adjust. Dose changes should always be directed by your prescriber.

GIP

A second incretin hormone released by the gut after eating. Tirzepatide targets both GLP-1 and GIP receptors, which may explain its larger average effect in clinical trials compared with GLP-1-only medicines.

Satiety Signaling

The brain-based process of feeling satisfied and no longer hungry. GLP-1 activates satiety receptors in the hypothalamus, the part of the brain that regulates appetite and energy balance.

How It Works in Your Body

4 Ways GLP-1 Changes Your Biology

GLP-1 receptor agonists work on multiple systems at once. That's why the results can be more significant than traditional approaches.

Appetite Suppression

GLP-1 activates receptors in the hypothalamus that reduce hunger signals. Many people report thinking about food far less often, even with smaller portions.

Slowed Digestion

GLP-1 slows how quickly your stomach empties after eating. You feel full longer and experience fewer blood sugar spikes, which may reduce cravings for high-carb foods.

Improved Insulin Response

GLP-1 stimulates the pancreas to release insulin, mainly in response to elevated blood sugar. This glucose-dependent action may reduce the risk of hypoglycemia (low blood sugar).

Reduced Glucagon

GLP-1 suppresses glucagon, the hormone that tells your liver to release stored sugar into the bloodstream. This helps prevent post-meal glucose surges.

Am I a Candidate?

Who May Benefit from GLP-1 Medicines?

GLP-1 medicines aren't right for everyone, and that's by design. A licensed clinician reviews your full health picture before any prescription is issued.

May Qualify

Weight Management

Wegovy and Zepbound are FDA-approved for adults who meet specific clinical thresholds and want medically supervised weight management.

  • BMI ≥ 30 (obesity classification)
  • BMI ≥ 27 with a weight-related condition (e.g., high blood pressure, high cholesterol, sleep apnea)
  • Used alongside a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity
  • Seeking a clinically supervised, structured plan
May Qualify

Blood Sugar / Metabolic Health

GLP-1 medicines were originally developed for type 2 diabetes and remain an evidence-based tool for blood sugar control.

  • Type 2 diabetes diagnosis
  • Prediabetes or elevated A1C (discuss with your clinician)
  • Insulin resistance or metabolic syndrome
  • Reviewing other diabetes medicines with your clinician
Discuss with Your Clinician

May Not Be Appropriate If…

GLP-1 medicines are not suitable for everyone. A clinician will review your history before prescribing.

  • Personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer
  • History of pancreatitis
  • Pregnancy or planning to become pregnant
  • Certain gastrointestinal conditions
  • Type 1 diabetes (different treatment pathway)

Want a Clinician's Opinion?

Talk to a licensed clinician, such as your primary care provider, an endocrinologist, an obesity medicine specialist or a licensed telehealth prescriber, about whether a GLP-1 medicine fits your health history and goals. A good clinician will tell you honestly if it isn't the right fit.

Treatment Journey

What to Expect Week by Week

GLP-1 treatment isn't an overnight fix, but the typical progression is well documented.

Weeks 1–4

Starting Low, Going Slow

Treatment usually begins at the lowest dose. Mild nausea may occur as your body adjusts and often improves over the first few weeks. Appetite reduction often begins within the first two weeks.

Weeks 4–12

Dose Titration & Habit Building

Your prescriber may increase the dose step by step. This is an important window for building the nutrition, activity and sleep habits that help make weight loss sustainable long-term.

Months 3–6

Meaningful Progress

In clinical studies, weight loss continues steadily over the first several months of treatment, along with improvements in blood sugar, blood pressure and other metabolic markers for many participants.

6+ Months

Maintenance & Review

At this stage, you and your prescriber review whether continued treatment, a dose change or another plan is appropriate. Plan for the long term from the start, including what happens if you stop.

Side Effects & Safety

GLP-1 Side Effects to Know About

Most side effects are digestive and happen early, while the dose is being increased. Knowing what's common, and what isn't, helps you have a better conversation with your clinician.

Common

Digestive Effects

These are the side effects people mention most, especially in the first few weeks.

  • Nausea
  • Constipation
  • Occasional vomiting
  • Fatigue during early weeks
Less Common

Serious but Rare

Serious side effects are uncommon, but they are the reason a clinician should review your history first.

  • Pancreatitis risk
  • Not appropriate with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer
  • Report severe or persistent symptoms to your clinician promptly
Lower the Risk

Practical Habits

Simple steps that support a smoother start, alongside your clinician's instructions.

  • Follow the prescribed titration schedule
  • Tell your prescriber about every medicine and supplement you take
  • Use only FDA-approved products dispensed by a licensed pharmacy
  • Keep follow-up appointments
Cost & Insurance Basics

What GLP-1 Medicines Cost

Cost is one of the biggest questions people have. Here are the basics to discuss with your insurer and prescriber.

List prices are high. Brand-name options like Wegovy or Zepbound can cost $900–$1,300/month without insurance.

Coverage varies. A plan may cover a GLP-1 medicine for type 2 diabetes but not for weight management, and many plans require prior authorization. Coverage depends on your plan, diagnosis and the specific brand.

Ask before you start. Call your insurer, ask which GLP-1 brands are on your plan's formulary, and ask your prescriber's office whether prior authorization is needed.

Be Prepared

Questions to Ask Your Prescriber

  1. Am I a good candidate for a GLP-1 medicine, based on my BMI and health history?
  2. Which brand do you recommend for me (for example Wegovy, Zepbound, Ozempic or Mounjaro), and why?
  3. What dose will I start on, and how will my titration schedule work?
  4. Which side effects should I expect, and which ones mean I should call you?
  5. Do any of my current medicines or conditions change your recommendation?
  6. Is it covered by my insurance, and is prior authorization required?
  7. How often will we follow up, and how will we measure progress?
  8. What is the long-term plan, including what happens if I stop?
